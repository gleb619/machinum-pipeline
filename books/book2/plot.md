# The Quiet Frequency — Plot and Chapter Outline

## Premise

Nadia Okonkwo is a sound archivist at the Marrow Bay Municipal Archive, a damp basement
of an old Carnegie library on Fenwick Street. Twelve years ago her older brother Emeka, a
night engineer at a failing AM radio station, vanished from a fishing boat called the
*Pelargic*. The official report said he drowned in a storm; no body was ever found.

Three weeks before the story opens, Cormac Doyle — the boat's owner — dies of a heart
attack. His daughter Fiona donates his boxes of papers and tapes to the archive. In them
Nadia finds a quarter-inch reel labeled "KSTL 3/3/14 — weather log." The station was
supposed to be off the air that night. It was not.

On the tape: a distress call from a ship that officially sank weeks earlier, a man's voice
giving an order, and Emeka whispering her name — telling her not to look for him, but to
look at the water.

Nadia's search for the truth pulls her into the 2014 cover-up of a scuttled ship called the
*Anselm* and the death of a deckhand named Tobias Renn, into the path of a developer named
Desmond Rusk who wants the whole north harbor rebuilt and forgotten, and finally to a town
up the coast where a man called Elias Ward has been repairing boat radios for twelve years.

## Genre and tone

Modern literary mystery. Coastal Pacific Northwest atmosphere: rain, ferry horns in fog,
salt on windows, fluorescent archive lighting. Clean, restrained prose; no graphic violence,
no profanity. Mysteries are solved by listening, reading records, and asking the right
person the right question — not by action sequences. Short paragraphs, concrete sensory
detail, present-tense clarity inside a past-tense narration.

## Main characters

- **Nadia Okonkwo** — 34. Audio archivist. Methodical, insomniac, patient to a fault. Keeps
  a field notebook of sounds she has recorded. Lives in her late grandmother's narrow house
  on Salt Row. Hums under her breath when she is frightened. Divorced, no children. Drives a
  green Volvo wagon with a broken tape deck.
- **Emeka "Em" Okonkwo** — her older brother, 26 when he vanished in 2014, 38 now. Warm,
  reckless, a night owl. Taught Nadia to splice tape with a razor blade and a candle. After
  the night of 3 March 2014 he has lived as **Elias Ward**, a marine electronics repairman
  in Cape Verity, ninety miles up the coast.
- **Fiona Doyle** — 29. Marine biologist. Cormac's daughter. Direct, practical, grieving.
  Donated her father's boxes. Becomes Nadia's closest ally.
- **Gus Pemberton** — 61. Nadia's supervisor at the archive. Kind, rules-bound, eats licorice
  drops constantly. Was the KSTL engineer on duty the night of the storm; has carried the
  guilt for twelve years.
- **Desmond Rusk** — 45. Developer, Rusk Marine Holdings. Polished, patient, never raises his
  voice. Wants the North Pier redevelopment and the archive's waterfront records out of the
  way. His father Arthur gave the order in 2014.
- **Arthur Rusk** — Desmond's father, 79, in the Bellwether Hill care home, dementia. Repeats
  fragments: "eleven forty," "the water takes what it's given."
- **Detective Joaquin Serrano** — 52, Marrow Bay Police. Closed the 2014 case. Guilty, tired,
  quietly helpful once pushed.
- **Vera Lindqvist** — 78. Harbormaster in 2014. Now dying in hospice. Keeps a green glass
  float on her windowsill.
- **Adaeze Okonkwo** — 66. Nadia and Emeka's mother. Retired schoolteacher. Has refused to
  speak Emeka's name for twelve years.

## World facts (must stay consistent)

- City: **Marrow Bay**. Districts: **Salt Row** (working-class row houses, Nadia's home),
  **Bellwether Hill** (care home, old money), **the Narrows** (old harbor), **the Cinder
  Yards** (abandoned rail yard, meeting place), **North Pier** (Rusk redevelopment site).
- **Kestrel Point** and the decommissioned **KSTL-AM 1140** radio station, closed in 2014.
  Its transmitter room still holds a low-power emergency transmitter.
- The **Marrow Bay Municipal Archive**, basement of the Carnegie library, Fenwick Street.
  Gus Pemberton is the supervisor. The archive holds the harbor authority's records on
  permanent loan.
- The **Pelargic**: 42-foot trawler owned by Cormac Doyle. Found adrift and empty on the
  morning of 4 March 2014.
- The **Anselm**: a derelict freighter scuttled illegally in deep water in February 2014 for
  salvage fraud. Deckhand **Tobias Renn** died aboard; the death was ruled accidental.
- **Rusk Marine Holdings** is the developer. **Desmond Rusk** is its public face. His father
  **Arthur Rusk** was deputy harbormaster in 2014.
- Timeline: the past event is the night of **3 March 2014**. The present action runs over
  roughly three weeks in **late autumn 2026**.
- Motifs: tape hiss; the number **1140**; the ferry horn in fog; a **green glass float**;
  the phrase **"look at the water."**
- The archive's old reel-to-reel deck is a **Revox A77**; Nadia digitizes to a laptop with a
  borrowed interface.

## Act I — Discovery (chapters 1-7)

1. **The Donation** — Fiona Doyle brings her father's boxes to the archive. Nadia catalogs
   them and finds a biscuit tin holding a quarter-inch reel labeled "KSTL 3/3/14 — weather
   log." She recognizes her brother's handwriting on the label, though she says nothing to
   Fiona. The chapter establishes the archive, her routine, and the twelve-year-old absence.
2. **Hiss** — Nadia threads the reel on the Revox after hours. She hears a distress call from
   the *Anselm*, a man's voice ordering silence, and Emeka's whisper naming her and telling
   her to look at the water. Gus appears in the doorway and reacts badly; she decides not to
   log the tape in the official register.
3. **Salt Row** — At home, Nadia digs out the 2014 newspaper clippings and the case file she
   was given years ago. She visits her mother Adaeze, who refuses to discuss Emeka and warns
   her to leave the past alone. Nadia copies the tape to her laptop that night.
4. **The Weather Log** — She checks KSTL's broadcast logs and finds the station listed as
   off-air for storm damage on 3 March. The contradiction proves the tape is real and the
   record has been altered. She asks Gus about it; he deflects and tells her to be careful.
5. **Kestrel Point** — Nadia drives up the coast road to the abandoned station. She finds the
   transmitter room, a hidden engineer's logbook, and evidence of recent activity. A Rusk
   Marine security truck arrives; she hides and leaves with the logbook.
6. **The Developer** — Desmond Rusk visits the archive with a donation offer and a request to
   transfer the waterfront records to his company for "safekeeping." He mentions Emeka by
   name and suggests the family would be better off with the past left alone.
7. **Cleaning the Reel** — Fiona comes to the archive; together they digitize the tape and
   filter the hiss. Underneath the storm they isolate a second voice: "Put it down, Okonkwo."
   Fiona realizes her father knew. Act I ends with the two women deciding to keep going.

## Act II — Escalation (chapters 8-15)

8. **The Detective** — Nadia takes the tape to Detective Serrano, who closed the case. He is
   cold at first, then admits pages are missing from the 2014 file and that the *Anselm* was
   never properly investigated. He warns her that Rusk has friends.
9. **The Anselm** — In the archive's shipping records Nadia reconstructs the *Anselm*: a
   freighter stripped and scuttled for insurance and salvage fraud. She finds Tobias Renn's
   name in a casualty list and a settlement paid quietly to his mother.
10. **The Break-In** — Nadia's office is ransacked and the original reel is stolen; her laptop
    survives because she took it home. Gus admits he knew Emeka and was on duty that night.
    Her job is threatened by a complaint from Rusk Marine.
11. **Gus's Story** — Gus tells her what he heard on 3 March 2014: the distress call, the
    order to stay off the air, and Emeka's decision to keep the tape. He gives her the
    station's master log, which proves the station was broadcasting.
12. **North Pier** — Nadia walks the North Pier at night and finds the mooring where the
    *Pelargic* was found. An old fisherman, **Tomas Bel**, warns her that Doyle always said
    the boat did not sink — it was left. A ferry horn sounds in the fog as she leaves.
13. **The Care Home** — She visits Arthur Rusk at Bellwether Hill. His dementia loosens his
    tongue: "eleven forty," "the water takes what it's given." Desmond arrives; the threat
    becomes explicit — her job, her mother's house, her brother's memory.
14. **Cape Verity** — An old repair invoice in Doyle's boxes leads her ninety miles north to
    a shop called Ward Marine Electronics. She sees Emeka and he sees her; he bolts. She
    leaves her phone number under the door and drives home certain he is alive.
15. **The Green Float** — A green glass float appears on her porch with a note giving a time
    and a place: the Cinder Yards. It is the signal she and Emeka used as children. Act II
    ends with her going to meet him.

## Act III — Truth and resolution (chapters 16-21)

16. **Cinder Yards** — Nadia and Emeka meet among the rusted rails. He tells her the whole
    night: the *Anselm* distress call, Arthur Rusk's order, Cormac Doyle's help in faking his
    death. He stayed away to protect her and Adaeze. She is furious and relieved at once.
17. **The Confession** — Nadia visits Vera Lindqvist in hospice. The dying harbormaster
    confesses on Nadia's recorder: the scuttling, the payment to the Renn family, Arthur
    Rusk's order, and the order to bury the *Pelargic* inquiry.
18. **The Second Tape** — Emeka gives Nadia the master reel he kept hidden for twelve years.
    Combined with Vera's confession, it is enough. But Desmond Rusk schedules the demolition
    of Kestrel Point for the following dawn, which would destroy the transmitter and the last
    physical evidence.
19. **The Storm** — A storm closes the coast road. Nadia, Fiona, Gus, and Serrano drive to
    Kestrel Point to retrieve the station's master log and reach the emergency transmitter
    before the demolition crew. Power fails; Gus restores the generator; they get inside.
20. **Eleven Forty** — Nadia broadcasts the tape and Vera's confession on 1140 AM using the
    emergency transmitter. The city hears it. Desmond Rusk arrives to stop them; Serrano
    arrests him and the 2014 file is reopened. The truth is public.
21. **The Quiet Frequency** — Weeks later. Emeka comes home to Adaeze. Nadia catalogs the tape
    properly and logs it in the register at last. The archive is quiet; the ferry horn sounds
    through the fog; the hiss on the tape is just hiss now.
