# Chapter 5: The Cipher of Moonbell

Morning broke clear over Thornhaven, the storm spent and the streets washed clean. Sir Aldric broke his fast on bread and honey in the courier's hall, then sought out Master Gareth once more. The workshop smelled of linseed oil and iron gall. Gareth sat hunched over a desk, surrounded by loose sheets of parchment covered in strange symbols.

"You returned," Gareth said without looking up. "Good. I need a witness, and you are the only courier I trust."

Aldric closed the door. "A witness to what?"

Gareth gestured to the sheets. "Seraphine's ink reveals hidden text beneath ordinary writing. I have been translating the duke's correspondence for three weeks. The king's letter is not the only message Valerius carries." He paused. "There is a second letter, sealed within the first, written in a cipher only the moonbell ink can expose."

Aldric's hand drifted to his sword. "What does it say?"

Gareth slid a decoded sheet across the desk. The handwriting was Seraphine's, unmistakable in its elegant loops. Aldric read slowly, his lips moving. The letter spoke of a conspiracy among the crimson-robed advisors at Blackwater Reach — a plot to seize the autumn council and betray the king's trust. The name of the chief conspirator was Lord Edmund, a man Aldric had never met but whose reputation for ambition preceded him everywhere.

"Lord Edmund," Aldric breathed. "He commands the northern garrison."

"Precisely," Gareth said. "And the garrison marches south in twelve days."

Aldric straightened. "Then I must ride to the king."

"Not yet," Gareth replied, raising a hand. "First, we need proof beyond this cipher. Seraphine has arranged for a courier of her own — a woman called Elowen, skilled in languages and disguise. She will arrive from Lys within the week. Together, you will carry the decoded evidence to His Majesty."

Aldric studied the lean man's face. "And if Lord Edmund learns of this?"

Gareth smiled thinly. "Then we had better be swift."

That afternoon, Aldric visited the royal archive and spoke with the archivist, Brother Cassian, a tonsured monk with ink on his fingers. Cassian confirmed that the king's original letter had been logged correctly, but that a second, unregistered seal had been noticed and quietly set aside.

"Set aside by whom?" Aldric asked.

"By a scribe named Brother Aldous," Cassian replied. "He has since vanished."

Aldric felt the weight of the road ahead settle upon his shoulders. New names — Seraphine, Gareth, Mira, Tomlin, Elowen, Cassian, Aldous, Edmund — each a thread in a web he was only beginning to see. He returned to Elara and rode out toward the northern road, carrying not one message now but several, and the knowledge that the journey from Thornhaven was far from over.

Behind him, the city gates closed with a sound like a book shutting.
