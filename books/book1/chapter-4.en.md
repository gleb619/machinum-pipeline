# Chapter 4: The Autumn Council at Blackwater

Sir Aldric did not rest long. At dawn, a steward knocked on the south guest chamber door and summoned him to the duke's private study. Duke Valerius stood by a window, gazing across the misted promontory of Blackwater Reach. Beside him stood Seraphine of Lys, the duke's eldest daughter, her hair the colour of autumn copper and her eyes sharp as a falcon's.

"Sir Aldric," the duke said without turning. "My reply is sealed. Yet I have a second errand, and it is not for the king's ears alone."

Seraphine stepped forward. "You are to carry this," she said, pressing a slender silver vial into his hand. "It contains ink of my own making, distilled from the root of the moonbell flower. Deliver it to Master Gareth in Thornhaven. He will know its purpose."

Aldric frowned. "And the king's letter?"

"Carried as before," Valerius replied. "But pause first at the hamlet of Willowfen. There you will find a woman named Mira, a healer who once saved my son's life. Tell her the duke remembers, and that the debt is not forgotten."

Aldric bowed. "Your Grace, I shall honour both."

He descended to the courtyard, where Elara waited, restless and stamping. A stablehand named Tomlin, a boy of no more than fourteen, held the mare's reins. "She's been fed and watered, sir," Tomlin said, his voice cracking. "She'll carry you well."

Aldric thanked him and rode out. The road south wound through the marshlands of Willowfen, where reeds whispered and the air smelled of stagnant water. At a crooked cottage, he found Mira — small, grey-haired, her hands stained with herbs. She listened in silence as he delivered the duke's message, then pressed a bundle of dried yarrow into his palm.

"For the road," she said. "And for the mare."

By evening, Aldric reached the outskirts of Thornhaven. The city gates were lit by torches, and the guards recognised him. He made his way through the cobblestone square toward Master Gareth's workshop, the silver vial weighing lightly in his satchel beside the duke's sealed reply.

Gareth, a lean man with ink-stained fingers and a squinting gaze, received the vial with trembling hands. "Seraphine's ink," he murmured. "Then the cipher is complete at last."

Aldric asked no further questions. He delivered the king's letter to the royal archive, stabled Elara, and sat alone in his quarters. The sealed parchment, the moonbell ink, the healer's yarrow — all of it wove together into a tapestry he could not yet read. Somewhere beyond the walls, the storm from Blackwater had finally arrived, and rain hammered the shutters like a thousand impatient fingers.

He slept, but not deeply.
